Work gets duplicated, settings drift between sessions and nobody is sure who owns the reminders. The short answer to "how do teams manage regular webinars?" is this. Give every person their own login, name one owner per webinar, and start each new webinar as a copy of the last good one.

01 · Guide

Where team webinar management fits

Picture the third week of the month. Four webinars are booked for next month and three people are building them. Two of them have just set up the same registration page from scratch. That is the moment a team needs a way to run regular webinars without duplicated work.

What it's for

Team webinar management is the set of habits and settings that lets several people run webinars every month without stepping on each other. It covers who has access, who owns each webinar, and how a proven setup gets reused.

How it helps

  • Each person has their own login. You can tell who did what, and more than one host can be in the live room.
  • Roles match the work. Admins handle billing and settings, while Users build and run webinars.
  • Proven setups get reused. A clone carries the pages, schedule, polls, offers and emails into the next webinar.
  • Webinars are easy to find. Type filters, search and archiving keep a long list short.

What happens without it

Without it, one login gets shared and nobody can say who changed the reminder timing. Each webinar is built from nothing, so small differences creep in between sessions. Last quarter's webinars clutter the list, and the replay link goes out from the wrong one.

Why marketing and events leads care

It matters most to the lead who signs off the calendar and answers for the numbers. It starts to bite once you run more than two webinars a month, or once a new colleague joins and needs access on day one.

02 · Our approach

The EasyWebinar approach to webinar management

Take a typical case. A five-person marketing team at a construction project software company runs four webinars a month. They are a product demo, an onboarding session for new customers, a partner briefing and a topical panel. Each has a different owner, and two outside guests present every month.

The usual workaround is one shared login and a spreadsheet of who does what. That tends to work until two people edit the same webinar, or a guest needs a password just to present for 40 minutes. Each new webinar is then rebuilt by hand, and settings that worked last month are often forgotten.

What changed is the volume. A team running four webinars a month builds around 48 a year, so any step done by hand is done 48 times. Access and reuse stop being admin and start deciding whether the calendar holds.

EasyWebinar gives each teammate their own login with an Admin or User role, set by the Owner or an Admin. Guests join a single webinar with a Co-presenter or Moderator link, with no account needed. It clones a webinar with the parts you choose. It can also change the copy's type on the way, so a live session can become evergreen.

You can check the clone choices on the page: registration pages, the schedule, replay settings, embed widgets, polls, offers, email notifications and integration settings. How those choices stand against other platforms is covered in How EasyWebinar compares for webinar management for teams, below.

03 · How it works

How teams divide webinar work

There are four common ways a team splits the work of running webinars every month. Each one catches some problems and misses others.

One shared login

Everyone signs in with the same email and password. It is quick to set up, but you can't tell who changed what, and only one of you can host in the live room. When someone leaves, the password has to change for everyone.

One person builds everything

A single coordinator builds every webinar and hands it to the presenter. This keeps setups consistent, but that person becomes the bottleneck. A holiday or a sick day stops the whole calendar.

A named owner per webinar

Each webinar has one owner who builds it, checks it and answers for it, while others help in set roles. The UK government's service manual makes the same point about service teams: one person should have the decision-making authority to deliver on all aspects of a project. This catches the "I thought you sent it" gap, but it still needs everyone to have access.

A master webinar to copy

The team keeps one well-built webinar for each format and starts every new one as a copy. This stops settings drifting between sessions. It misses nothing on its own, but it only works if the master is kept up to date.

What EasyWebinar confirms

EasyWebinar supports the last two directly. Each teammate gets their own login and role, presenters and moderators can be added per webinar, and Clone Webinar copies a webinar with the parts you tick.

04 · Guide

Who does what: roles on the team

A team member is someone with a login on your account. A presenter or moderator is someone running one specific webinar, and they can join with a link without an account. Getting this difference right saves seats and keeps access tidy.

RoleWhere you set itWhat it allowsGood fit for
OwnerFixed, one per accountThe account holderThe person who holds the account
AdminSettings, then TeamFull access, including all settingsThe marketing lead, or whoever handles billing
UserSettings, then TeamBasic access to organisation featuresBuilders, presenters and moderators on your staff
PresenterThe webinar's Team & Moderators panel, or a join linkAppears on camera and presents in that webinarA guest speaker on one session
ModeratorThe Moderator Join Link for Live roomHandles chat and questions in that webinarA colleague or guest who runs the Q&A queue

Give Admin only to people who need to change billing, team or account settings. Everyone else who builds or runs webinars can be a User. The full set-up is on team members and roles.

Guests who present once

Send a one-off guest the Co-presenter Join Link from the webinar's Share dialog rather than adding them to your team. Treat that link as private, because anyone holding it can join your room in that role. The links meant for your audience are covered on sharing links.

When an agency runs webinars for clients

If your team runs webinars for clients, a client workspace keeps each client's webinars, registrants and settings apart. Each workspace runs on its own plan, and adding one doesn't use a team seat. If you run webinars only for your own business, team members and seats cover what you need.

05 · Guide

Keep a master webinar for each format

Cloning copies a webinar you've already built, so the next one starts from a setup that worked. It is also how you change a webinar's type, because type is fixed once a webinar exists. The steps are on clone a webinar.

GroupWhat a copy can include
Webinar assetsRegistration pages, webinar schedule, replay settings and embed widgets
EngagementPolls, offers and email notifications
IntegrationsIntegration settings

Registrants and attendees stay with the original, so a clone always starts at zero. That is what you want, because it is a new webinar rather than a continuation. Your original is left untouched.

Name the copy straight away

The copy's name is pre-filled with the original's name and "(Copy)". Rename it before you click Clone Webinar, using a pattern the whole team follows, such as format and month. Four "(Copy)" webinars in one list is how the wrong one gets edited.

Clone before you experiment

If you want to try a new registration page or offer on a webinar that's already running, clone it and change the copy. When a live session worked well, clone it as evergreen; it then needs a recording, which you pick on the new webinar's Content tab. That route is covered in turning a live webinar into an evergreen one.

06 · Guide

Find any webinar in a busy account

My Webinars lists everything your team has built. Once there are more than a handful, the filters, search and sort get you to the right one quickly.

The tabs across the top are All, Live, Simulive, Evergreen and Archived, and each shows a live count. Search finds a webinar by name, which is why the naming pattern matters. Sort By defaults to Newest First, and a toggle switches between grid and list views.

Every card shows the webinar's type, its name, the next session's date, time and timezone, and its registrant count. Webinars built with Create with AI also carry an AI badge, so the team can see which drafts still need a human edit.

Archive last month's webinars

There is no Delete. Webinars are archived, so the record of past events and their registrants stays intact. Archive each webinar once its follow-up has finished, and the main list then shows only what is live or coming up.

07 · Guide

See every registrant in one place

People in the sidebar lists every registrant and attendee across all your webinars in one table. Each row is one person on one webinar, with their status, lead score, watch percentage and whether they clicked your offer.

Add Filter narrows the list by Webinar, Dates, User, Behavior, Source & UTM or Device & Location. Filter first, then Export, so whoever runs follow-up gets just the group they need. The full filter list is on the People view.

An exported list is personal data, so agree who on the team may export and why. UK data protection law expects personal data to be used for specified, explicit purposes and kept for no longer than is necessary.

08 · Measure

How to measure whether a webinar is worth cloning

Two measures tell you whether a webinar's setup deserves to become next month's master. Both sit on each webinar's Analytics tab.

Visitors to registered

This rate shows your registration page doing its job. A low figure is a page problem: the headline, the form or the promise. Its weak spot is that Visitors counts unique IPs, so many people behind one office network can look like one visitor.

Registered to attended

This rate shows your reminders doing their job. A low figure is a notification problem, not a content one. Total Attended counts unique people, live or replay, so check the Live only, Replay only and Both split before you judge the live session.

Where to find them

Open each webinar's Analytics tab and note the two rates. To compare webinars, open them one at a time. Then fix the weak stage on the master before you clone it, using the registration page builder or reminder emails. Each figure is explained on webinar analytics.

09 · By hand

How to run regular webinars as a team by hand

Without a platform built for teams, the job looks like this:

  1. Keep a shared calendar of every webinar, with one owner's name on each.
  2. Share one login, or ask each person to set up their own tool.
  3. Copy last month's registration page, emails and polls into a new webinar by hand.
  4. Email guest presenters the account password, then change it afterwards.
  5. Download each webinar's registrant list and merge them in a spreadsheet.
  6. Delete or hide old webinars by hand so nobody edits the wrong one.

Steps 3 and 5 are where most mistakes happen, because they are repeated for every webinar.

The same job in EasyWebinar

The calendar and the owner's name stay with you. Everything else changes: each person gets their own login under Team, guests get a join link, Clone Webinar copies the setup, People holds every registrant in one list, and Archive clears old webinars from view.

11 · Set it up

Set up your team in EasyWebinar

Work through these steps once, then repeat steps 4 to 6 for each new webinar. The preparation checklist covers what to test before each session.

  1. Add each colleague. Click Settings, then Team, then Add user. Enter their name, email and a password, and choose Admin or User.
  2. Pass on the login yourself. Nothing is emailed when you add someone, so send them their details and ask them to change the password.
  3. Check your seats. The Settings overview shows how many seats you've used, such as 2 of 10.
  4. Pick the master. On My Webinars, open the card menu on the webinar that worked and choose Clone Webinar.
  5. Choose the type and parts. Pick Live, Simulive or Evergreen, rename the copy, tick what it should include, and click Clone Webinar.
  6. Add the session team. On the new webinar's Overview tab, use Team & Moderators to add presenters, or send guests their join links.

This video shows team roles being set up, then a custom domain added for your webinar pages.

Use your own domain

Custom domain (C-Name) points your own domain at your webinar pages, so links read like webinars.yourcompany.com. Add a CNAME record with your DNS provider first, then enter the domain on your profile. Every webinar the team builds then shares the same address.

Check the copy before anyone shares it

Open the new webinar and check its schedule, its reminder emails and its offer links. A clone carries the old settings, so a stale date or a last-month offer is the most common slip. Then send the registration link out.

16 · Limits

Limits to plan around

  • You set each teammate's password. There is no invitation to accept, so you pass the login on yourself.
  • The Owner can't be changed from the Team screen. Contact support if ownership needs to move.
  • Each member needs their own email address.
  • Seats are part of your plan. Adding people beyond your allowance costs more.
  • A webinar's type is fixed. To change it, clone the webinar and pick the new type on the copy.
  • Registrants stay with the original. A clone always starts with no registrations.
  • Webinars are archived, not deleted. They move to the Archived tab and keep their records.
  • Analytics are per webinar. You compare webinars by opening each one's Analytics tab. Defaults are listed in limits and defaults.
17 · Questions

Common questions about webinar management for teams

How do teams manage regular webinars?

Give every person their own login with the right role, and name one owner per webinar. Start each new webinar as a copy of the last good one, then archive old webinars once their follow-up ends.

What is webinar event management?

It is the work of planning, building, running and following up each webinar: access, pages, reminders, presenters and results. For a team it also means agreeing who owns each step, so nothing is done twice or missed.

How do I give a colleague access to EasyWebinar?

Click Settings, then Team, then Add user. Enter their name, email and a password, choose Admin or User, and send them the login yourself, because nothing is emailed.

What's the difference between Admin and User?

An Admin has full access, including settings and billing. A User gets basic access to organisation features, which suits people who build, present or moderate webinars without touching the account.

Does a guest presenter need an account?

No. Send them the Co-presenter Join Link from the webinar's Share dialog, and they join that webinar without a login. Keep the link private, because anyone holding it can join in that role.

Can a moderator join without a team seat?

Yes. The Moderator Join Link for Live room lets someone handle chat and questions in one webinar without being added to your team, so they don't use a seat.

How many team members can I add?

It depends on your plan. The Settings overview shows how many seats you've used out of your allowance, and adding people only costs more once that allowance is used up.

How do I reuse a webinar that worked?

Open the card menu on My Webinars and choose Clone Webinar. Pick the type, rename the copy, tick the parts to include, such as pages, polls, offers and emails, and click Clone Webinar.

Does cloning copy registrants?

No. Registrants and attendees stay with the original webinar, so the copy starts with no registrations. The original webinar is not changed by cloning.

Can I change a live webinar to evergreen?

Not in place, because a webinar's type is fixed once created. Clone it and choose Evergreen as the copy's type, then pick a recording on the new webinar's Content tab.

How do I delete an old webinar?

You archive it instead. Open the webinar's card menu and choose Archive, and it moves to the Archived tab, out of your main list, with its records kept.

Where can I see registrants from every webinar?

Click People in the sidebar. It lists every registrant and attendee across all your webinars, and Add Filter narrows the list by webinar, dates, behaviour, source or device before you export.

Can agencies keep each client's webinars apart?

Yes. A client workspace gives each client a separate account with its own plan, so webinars, registrants and settings stay apart. Adding a workspace doesn't use one of your team seats.

Can the team's webinars run on our own domain?

Yes. Add a CNAME record with your DNS provider, then enter the domain under Custom domain (C-Name) on your profile, so webinar links use your company's address.
