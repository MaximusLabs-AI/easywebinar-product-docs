Webinar Setup Checklist: What to Confirm, Test and Set Before You Press Go Live

A webinar setup checklist for the days before and the hour before a live session: what to confirm, what to test in the green room, and what to set so nothing breaks once you go live.

Applies to
Hosts and producers preparing a live webinar · live webinars in the live room

A webinar setup checklist is the list you work through before a live session so that the camera, the sound, the links and the timed pieces all work when people arrive. Most of what goes wrong on the day can be caught in a test run a day or two before, and the rest in the last half hour. This page gives you a fill-in checklist for both, says what each check catches, and shows where each one sits in EasyWebinar's live room, which lets you test privately before anything is broadcast.

Where a webinar setup checklist fits

Picture the hour before a live session. The host is sorting slides, a co-host is still looking for the join link, and nobody has checked that the room is recording.

What it's for

A webinar setup checklist turns that hour into a fixed order of checks. It covers testing and set-up only, not what you will say. For planning the session itself, the webinar content planning checklist on the EasyWebinar blog covers topics, structure and slides.

How it helps

  • Problems surface while they are private. You find a muted microphone in the green room, not in the chat.
  • Each person knows their part. Co-hosts join backstage at a time you set, with their own link.
  • The timed pieces fire. Reminders, polls and offers are checked before they are needed.
  • The ending is planned. Attendees are sent to a page you chose, and the recording is waiting in your library.

What happens without it

Without a fixed list, checks happen in whatever order you remember them. The ones you forget tend to be the ones you can't fix live: an empty redirect, a co-host without a link, or recording switched off. Those cost you the replay, the next step for attendees, or the first five minutes of the session.

Why customer success teams care

Customer success teams run the same onboarding or release webinar again and again, often with a different presenter each time. A shared checklist means the session works the same way whoever runs it. It matters most on the first run with a new co-host, and when the team is presenting to its largest customers.

The EasyWebinar approach to webinar setup checklists

Take a typical case. A four-person customer success team at an accounting software company runs a live onboarding webinar for new customers every month. One manager hosts, and a product specialist joins as co-host to answer setup questions in the chat.

The usual way to prepare is a private video call the day before, with the presenters talking through their slides. That can catch a bad microphone. It probably won't catch what attendees see, or whether the reminders arrive. Nor will it show where people land when the session ends.

What changed is simple. The checks that matter most happen inside the webinar platform itself. So the best test is the real room, set up for the real session, before anyone is watching.

EasyWebinar builds that test into the way you enter a live session. It asks you to check your camera, microphone and speakers before you reach the green room. It then keeps you in a green room where nothing is broadcast. Co-hosts wait backstage until you bring them on. Polls and offers can be switched on and off without deleting them, and reminders can be timed relative to the start.

How webinar setups get tested

There are four common ways to test a webinar before going live. Each catches some problems and misses others, which is why the checklist uses more than one.

A solo device check

You open the room on your own and look at your camera, your microphone level and your speakers. This catches the most common failure, a device the browser can't use. In Chrome, for example, a site that isn't allowed your camera or microphone won't work for video calls, and on a work computer your network administrator may control those settings. It misses everything attendees see.

A full test run with a colleague as attendee

You run the session end to end, with a colleague registered as an ordinary attendee on a second device. This catches registration, reminders, the attendee view, polls and the redirect at the end. It takes the most time, so do it a day or two before, not on the day.

A test send of each message

You send each reminder or text message to yourself before any registrant gets it. This catches wrong times, broken links and messages that read badly on a phone. It misses whether the room itself works.

A check from a phone

You open the attendee view on a phone, or in a narrow browser window. This catches slides and shared screens that are too small to read. It matters because attendees on phones, and in browser windows narrower than 768 pixels, can't zoom in on what you share.

The days before: confirm and set

The week-before and two-days-before parts of the checklist happen outside the room. These are the settings you can't change once the session has started without disturbing it.

Send each co-host their own link

Every webinar's Overview tab has its own share links, including a Co-presenter Join Link and a Moderator Join Link for Live room. Send each person the link for their role, and keep the Attendee Join Link for registrants. A co-host who joins on an attendee link watches the session instead of joining it. The sharing links page lists every link and who it is for.

Time the reminders around the start

Reminders are set as a time before the webinar, such as one hour or 15 minutes before. A common pattern is three: a day before, an hour before and 15 minutes before. Because the timing is relative to the start, it still works if you move the date.

This video shows where reminder emails are set and how their timing works.

If you send text reminders, SMS (Beta) needs the Phone field on your registration form, added in the registration page builder, or there is nobody to text. The reminder emails page covers each setting, and confirmation emails covers the first message registrants get.

Fill in where attendees go at the end

The End of Session and Expired Session boxes on the General tab are empty by default. Left empty, attendees sit on a finished webinar with nowhere to go. Paste your offer, booking page or thank-you page into End of Session, do the same for Expired Session, and click Save Changes.

This video shows how to set the redirects and add tracking code to the webinar.

Decide what attendees can see and say

Two settings decide what attendees can do, and both are set before the session. Show Attendees on the webinar's Settings tab controls whether the attendee list is visible. Chat is set on the Engagement tab, where Hosts only makes every message private to your team and No Chat removes the panel. The live chat page explains each option.

The test run: what to try and in what order

Run the full test a day or two before, so there is time to fix what it finds. Use the real webinar, not a copy, so the links and settings are the ones attendees will get.

Test as an attendee, not only as the host

Ask a colleague to register with their own email address on a different device. They should receive the registration confirmation and the reminders you set, and each link should open the right page. Their device is also where you check the attendee view of your slides and polls.

Send yourself each text message

If you use SMS (Beta), select a template, click Send Test SMS, pick your country from the flag dropdown and type your number. Choose the country from the list rather than typing a dialling code. The message arrives from your EasyWebinar number, exactly as a registrant would see it. WhatsApp (Beta) messages can be tested on your own number in the same way.

Preview every poll and offer

On the CTAs tab, the poll editor previews each poll exactly as attendees will see it. Use the toggle on each row to keep an offer ready but switched off until you need it. On a live webinar you launch polls by hand, so decide in the test run who launches each one and when. The polls and offers pages cover the settings.

Test AI answers before anyone sees them

If you use AI moderation, open Test Chat Moderation and ask it the hard questions your customers ask. It opens a private conversation, so nothing reaches attendees. It retrains each time you add a document to its knowledge base, so give it a moment after an upload before you test.

Make slides readable without zooming in

Attendees watching on a phone can't zoom in on a shared screen or a shared file. The same is true in any browser window narrower than 768 pixels, which the room treats as a phone. So the real test is whether your slides can be read at the size a phone shows them.

Small text is the first thing to go. Nielsen Norman Group's guidance on legibility says that tiny text dooms legibility, and that text needs high contrast between characters and background. On a slide, that means fewer words in larger type, dark text on a plain light background or the reverse, and no fine print in the corners.

To check, open your slides on your own phone at the size they fill the screen. If you have to squint, enlarge the type or split the slide in two. Do the same for any screen you plan to share, such as a spreadsheet or a product screen: zoom your own browser in before you share it, so the text is large when it arrives.

How to run a webinar test by hand

Without a private room, a test run takes more people and more steps. This is the manual method many teams use.

  1. Book a 30-minute call with every presenter a day or two before.
  2. Ask each presenter to join from the computer and browser they will use on the day.
  3. Have each one turn on their camera and microphone, and fix any the browser blocks.
  4. Ask each presenter to share their screen and show their first slide.
  5. Register a colleague with their own email, and wait for the reminders to arrive.
  6. Check every link in each reminder by hand.
  7. Agree who speaks when, who answers questions and who ends the session.
  8. On the day, join early and repeat steps 3 and 4.

The weak point is that the call isn't the webinar, so the replay page and the redirect go untested too. A microphone that works in one tool may still need permission in another, and nothing in the call tests the attendee view.

The same job in EasyWebinar

In EasyWebinar, steps 3, 4 and 8 happen in the live room itself. From the webinar's Overview, click Enter Room, check your devices, and continue to the green room. Your co-hosts join backstage on their own link and sort out their audio there. A guest co-host needs only a join link, not an account, while regular presenters can be added as team members. Steps 5 and 6 stay the same, and SMS (Beta) adds a test send to your own number.

Set up and test in EasyWebinar

Work through these screens in order on the day, starting about half an hour before the start.

Check your camera, microphone and speakers

From the webinar's Overview, click Enter Room. You're asked to check your camera, microphone and speakers, then click Continue to Green Room.

The button stays greyed out until the room can see both a camera and a microphone. You need both to enter, even if you turn them off afterwards. If your camera isn't found, the camera not detected page walks through the fixes.

Use the green room for your last private checks

The green room header reads Not Live, and nothing here reaches your audience. Check your camera framing, test your screen share with Share, and get your co-hosts in place before you press anything.

The side rail holds Chat, People, Offers, Polls, Design, Question and Theme. Open Polls and Offers now, so you know where they are when you need them. The green room and backstage page describes each control.

Confirm every presenter is backstage

Co-hosts and moderators land backstage, not on stage, and your audience doesn't see or hear them until you bring them on. Open People to see the presenters, with the host marked, and the attendees.

If someone is missing, use Invite People to share their join link again. Move each person to the stage when it is their turn, and back when they're done.

Check recording and moderation

The header shows Auto Recording Enabled when recording is on. To change it, or to check your devices and moderation, click Settings.

Recorded sessions land in the Library, under Recordings. Leave recording on unless you have a reason not to, because the replay is often watched by more people than the live session. The automatic recording page shows where to find it afterwards.

Press Go Live

Go Live asks you to confirm, and reminds you that anyone in the waiting area will be moved in automatically and that offers and polls should be ready. Once live, the header shows LIVE with a timer, and Go Live becomes End Session. Ending disconnects everyone and nobody can rejoin, so end only when you're finished.

If you stream to YouTube or LinkedIn

EasyCast, on Growth plans and above (see pricing), streams a live webinar to YouTube, LinkedIn or a custom RTMP destination at the same time. Connect each destination the day before. For YouTube, connect the Google account that actually owns the channel.

How to tell your test run passed

A test run passes when the colleague who played the attendee could do everything a real attendee will. Two measures tell you most of it.

The first is whether every message arrived. Count the confirmation and reminders your colleague received against the number you set up. If you set three reminders and two arrived, look at the timing of the missing one before the day. This only tells you about one inbox, so it can't show whether messages land in other people's spam folders.

The second is whether every step worked from the attendee's side. Your colleague should have entered the room, seen your slides, answered a poll and been sent to your redirect at the end. Ask them to read one line from your smallest slide off their phone. If they can't, your slides fail the phone check.

After the real session, the webinar analytics page shows how many registrants attended, which tells you whether the reminders did their job.

Worked example: how big your slide text looks on a phone

This is an illustrative calculation with sample figures, not an EasyWebinar output. Shared slides arrive as images about 1,500 pixels wide. When a phone shows the whole slide across a screen 375 pixels wide, every part of it shrinks to a quarter of its size.

Text on the slideHeight on the slideShown 375 pixels wide (1,500 ÷ 375 = 4)Shown 750 pixels wide (1,500 ÷ 750 = 2)
Footnote20 pixels20 ÷ 4 = 5 pixels20 ÷ 2 = 10 pixels
Body text40 pixels40 ÷ 4 = 10 pixels40 ÷ 2 = 20 pixels
Heading80 pixels80 ÷ 4 = 20 pixels80 ÷ 2 = 40 pixels

On the phone, the footnote ends up a quarter of its slide size, and the attendee can't zoom in to read it. Size every line of text you need people to read as if it will be shown four times smaller, and cut the rest.

Limits of testing in the live room

  • Polls are launched by hand on a live webinar. Plan who launches each one, because they don't appear on their own.
  • A camera and a microphone are required to enter the room, even if you turn them off once you're in.
  • Ending a session can't be undone. End Session disconnects everyone, and nobody can rejoin.
  • Attendees on phones can't zoom in on a shared screen or file, and nor can anyone in a browser window narrower than 768 pixels.
  • AI moderation needs the built-in chat. Choosing a third-party chat tool turns it off.

Common questions about webinar setup

What should I check before a webinar?

Check your camera, microphone and speakers, your screen share, your co-hosts' join links, your reminders, your polls and offers, and where attendees go when it ends. Check recording last, in the room, before you go live.

How long before a webinar should I test my setup?

Run a full test a day or two before, so there is time to fix what you find. Then enter the room about half an hour before the start for the device check and the green room.

How do I prepare for a webinar on the day?

Enter the room, pick your camera, microphone and speakers, and continue to the green room. Check your framing and screen share, make sure co-hosts are backstage and recording is on, then go live.

Is anything in the green room broadcast?

No. The header reads Not Live until you click Go Live, and nothing in the green room reaches your audience. It is the place for your last private checks.

Why can't I get into the green room?

The Continue to Green Room button stays greyed out until the room can see both a camera and a microphone. Allow both in your browser, pick them on the device check, and try again.

Where do co-hosts appear when they join?

Co-hosts and moderators land backstage, not on stage. Your audience doesn't see or hear them until you move them to the stage, and you can move them back when they're done.

Can attendees zoom in on my slides?

Not on a phone, and not in a browser window narrower than 768 pixels. Make your slides readable at phone size without zooming in, and test them on your own phone the day before.

Can I send myself a test reminder?

For SMS (Beta), yes: select a template and click Send Test SMS. WhatsApp (Beta) can be tested on your own number too. For email reminders, register with your own address and check what arrives.

How many reminders should I set?

A common pattern is three: a day before, an hour before and 15 minutes before. Each reminder's timing is set relative to the start, so it still works if the date moves.

How do I keep an offer ready but hidden?

On the CTAs tab, turn the toggle on that offer's row off. The offer is kept but not shown, so you can switch it on when you reach it in the session.

Is my live webinar recorded automatically?

When recording is on, the header shows Auto Recording Enabled. You can change it under Settings, then Recording Settings, and the recording lands in your library under Recordings.

What happens if I forget the redirect?

The End of Session box is empty by default, so attendees stay on a finished webinar. Paste your next-step page into it on the General tab before the session and click Save Changes.

Can I test AI answers before a webinar?

Yes. Test Chat Moderation opens a private conversation with the AI moderator, so you can ask it what customers ask. Give it a moment after each new document, because it retrains.

Is this checklist built into EasyWebinar?

No. The checklist on this page is a template to copy into your own document or print. EasyWebinar's part is the device check, the green room, backstage and the test sends.
