A sales webinar for a small business is a free online session that teaches one useful thing and ends with one clear offer. If you've never run one, the hard part isn't presenting. It's getting the format, the registration page, the offer and the follow-up to work together while you do everything alone. Run your first one live, show your offer inside the room with a countdown, then email attendees and no-shows separately afterwards. EasyWebinar holds all four steps in one webinar, so you build them once and reuse them.

Where sales webinars fit for a small business

You've got a service or product that people buy once they understand it, and explaining it one call at a time is eating your week. A sales webinar lets you explain it once to a room of interested people, then make the offer while their interest is high.

What they're for

A sales webinar turns a list of curious people into buyers in one sitting. It teaches something useful first, so the offer at the end feels like the natural next step rather than a pitch.

How they help

  • One explanation for many buyers. You present once instead of repeating the same call.
  • The offer sits in the room. Attendees click a button while they're still watching.
  • The session keeps working. A live session can be cloned into an automated one that runs on a schedule.
  • Your contacts stay where you keep them, in your CRM or ours. Keep HubSpot, Salesforce Pardot (Account Engagement), ActiveCampaign, Mailchimp or Kit (formerly ConvertKit), or use EasyCRM natively.

What happens without them

Without a sales webinar, every sale needs its own conversation. Your time sets the ceiling on how many people you can sell to, and interested people who can't get a call slot drift away.

Why small business owners care

It matters most to owners and solo operators who sell something that takes ten minutes or more to explain. It matters most in the weeks before a launch or a seasonal push, when you need many conversations at once.

The EasyWebinar approach to your first sales webinar

Take a typical case. A one-person home organising business sells a paid decluttering package, and every new client has asked the same questions on a discovery call. The owner wants to answer those questions once, in a free 40-minute session, and invite the room to book.

The usual workaround is to string tools together. That often means a video-call link, a payment page, a spreadsheet of who signed up and a few emails sent by hand. Each piece works alone, but nobody tells you who watched, who clicked and who still needs the recording.

What changed is simple. A webinar no longer has to be live every time. Once you've presented it well, the recording can play at set times while the offer and follow-up run on their own.

It then sends different emails to attendees and no-shows, and it can turn the live session into an evergreen one by cloning it.

Live or automated: choosing your first format

Run your first sales webinar live. You'll hear which parts land, which questions come up and where people leave, and that tells you what to fix before you automate it. EasyWebinar offers three types, and you choose one when you create the webinar.

Live

You present in real time and attendees join at the scheduled moment. Choose live when the value is in you being there, such as a launch or a workshop. You can create and schedule a live webinar in four steps, and recording can be switched on so you keep the session.

Simulive

Simulive plays a recording at set times, with attendees registering for specific dates. Everyone in a session sees the same moment together, so the chat still works as a shared room. It suits a second run when you want to be in the chat but not on camera. The setup is covered in create a simulive webinar.

Evergreen

Evergreen makes the recording available around the clock, and each registrant picks a time that suits them. It suits an offer you sell all year. When your live session has gone well, you can turn it into an evergreen webinar by cloning it and choosing Evergreen on the copy.

What to check before you choose

The type can't be changed after you create the webinar. If you pick the wrong one, clone the webinar and choose the right type on the copy. Your original stays untouched, and its registrants stay with it.

How a sales webinar leads to the sale

Most sales webinars follow one of four patterns for where the offer goes. Each one catches some buyers and misses others, so pick the pattern before you write a word of your script.

The offer at the end

You teach for most of the session and introduce the offer in the last few minutes. It feels least pushy and builds trust with a cold audience. It misses anyone who leaves early, and on a first webinar that can be a large share of the room.

The offer woven through

You teach and mention the offer at natural points, then close with a clear invitation. People who leave halfway have still heard what you sell. The risk is that each mention interrupts the teaching if it isn't tied to what you just showed.

The direct pitch

The session centres on the offer, with repeated calls to act and strong urgency. It suits a warm audience that already knows you. It can feel heavy to people meeting you for the first time.

The offer after the session

You keep the session purely educational and make the offer in the follow-up emails. It suits a very cautious audience. The cost is time: a 2011 Harvard Business Review article found that most companies weren't responding to online leads nearly fast enough.

Write a short sales webinar script

A sales webinar script needs a hook and welcome, the problem your buyer has, what you teach, and the offer. Plan those sections before you build slides, using the webinar outline template if you want a structure to fill in. Write in the words your buyers use. Nielsen Norman Group's guidance on plain language says simple, familiar words help experts as well as beginners.

If you'd rather start from a draft, EasyWebinar can generate a sales webinar script from a description of your audience, your topic and your offer. The script comes back in named sections with timings and speaker notes. Read it before you move on, because the pages and emails are built from it.

Build a registration page that gets sign-ups

Your registration page is the link you promote, so it carries most of the work before the session. It needs a headline that names the result, three or four things people will learn, the date and time, and a form.

Keep the form short. Nielsen Norman Group's form design research says every field you cut raises the form's conversion rate. EasyWebinar's default form asks for Full name and Email only, and for a first webinar that's usually enough.

The registration page builder opens straight after you create the webinar, with your date, time and a live countdown already in place. Pick a template, then check the page in the Device toggle's mobile view, because many people will register from a phone. The page isn't public until you click Publish.

To invite people already on your list, send the One-Click Registration Link from sharing links. It carries their name and email in the URL, so a known contact registers without filling in the form.

Get people to turn up

Getting people to register is the easy part. Getting them to turn up is what reminders are for. A common pattern is three reminder emails: a day before, an hour before and fifteen minutes before. The last one does most of the work.

Reminder timing is set relative to the webinar, not a fixed clock time, so the same three reminders work on recurring and evergreen schedules. Set the sender name to one people recognise, and set the reply-to address to an inbox you read, because people do reply. An SMS reminder (Beta) can go alongside email, if you add the Phone field to your form.

Sell during the webinar

Your offer is a card with a headline and a button, shown in the room while people watch. Build it before the session on the CTAs tab, and keep its toggle off until you're ready to show it. This video shows an offer being made to attendees during a webinar, without sending them away first.

Set up the offer card

Write a headline that names what the buyer gets, then set the button. Button URL is where attendees land when they click: your checkout, booking page or sales page. Write the button Text to match what happens next, such as "Book your package", never "Click here". The steps are on the offers page.

Add a countdown

Switching on Countdown timer puts a countdown on the offer card, which makes the offer feel time-limited. Only use one if the deadline is real. The countdown timers page covers how it behaves.

Warm the room with a poll

Ask an easy question about the audience before you make the offer. A question such as "when are you planning to start?" also tells you who's ready to buy. Build it on the same tab, as described under polls.

If you charge to register

A sales webinar is usually free, but you can charge for a paid workshop instead. Attendees pay through a secure Stripe checkout, and the money goes straight to your Stripe account. EasyWebinar never holds your funds. The setup is under paid webinars.

Follow up with attendees and no-shows

Most of your registrants won't attend live, so the follow-up is where much of the selling happens. Send attendees a thank-you with the replay and the offer, and send no-shows the replay. Webinar Follow-ups go after the session, and you choose the audience for each: everyone who registered, only attendees, or only no-shows.

Turn on the replay page before you paste its link into an email. If the replay uses Webinar Replica, the same offers and polls carry over, so replay viewers can buy too. The replay pages guide covers the expiry options, and the full email set is on webinar follow-up emails.

For warmer leads, Attendee Action Follow-ups send based on what someone did, such as clicking your offer. In People, the filter for high watch time plus an offer click shows who was interested and didn't buy.

How to run a sales webinar by hand

You can run a first sales webinar with general tools. It takes more steps, and the gaps between tools are where people get lost.

  1. Write a one-page sign-up form in a form tool and collect names and emails.
  2. Create a meeting link in a video-call tool and paste it into a confirmation email.
  3. Send reminders by hand from your email tool the day before and an hour before.
  4. Present, then paste a payment link into the chat when you make the offer.
  5. Export the call's attendance list and match it against the sign-up sheet.
  6. Upload the recording somewhere and email the link to no-shows.
  7. Count who paid by checking your payment tool against the attendance list.

Measure your first sales webinar

Two measures tell you most of what a first sales webinar did. The first is how many registrants attended. The second is how many people who saw your offer clicked it and then bought.

On the Analytics tab, Visitors to Registered shows whether your registration page is doing its job. Registered to Attended shows whether your reminders are. Lower down, Views to Clicks and Clicks to Purchases separate the two offer problems. Low views to clicks means the offer isn't landing, and low clicks to purchases points to your checkout or pricing. The webinar analytics page explains each figure.

How far to trust these numbers

A first webinar has a small audience, so a few people change the percentages a lot. Compare one step with the next rather than with someone else's results. Replay views are counted separately from live attendance, so check Total Attended before you judge the session. If everything shows zero, widen the date range.

Common questions about sales webinars

Should a first sales webinar be live or automated?

Run your first sales webinar live. You'll learn which parts land and where people leave. Once it works, clone it in EasyWebinar and choose Evergreen, so the recording plays on a schedule with the same offers.

How do you sell on a webinar without being pushy?

Teach one useful thing first, then make one offer that follows from it. Show the offer card when the teaching reaches the problem your product solves. Use a countdown only when the deadline is real.

What should a sales webinar script include?

A sales webinar script needs a hook and welcome, the buyer's problem, what you teach and the offer. EasyWebinar's AI can draft one in named sections with timings and speaker notes, which you then edit.

What makes a good sales webinar topic?

Pick a topic that solves the first part of the problem your product solves. A home organiser might teach how to clear one room in a weekend, then offer the full package for the whole house.

How long should a sales webinar be?

In EasyWebinar you set the duration and slide count before AI writes the script, so decide how long you need to teach, make the offer and answer questions before you generate it.

Is there a sales webinar template I can start from?

EasyWebinar has registration page templates in categories such as Coach, Lead Magnet and Marketing. Its AI can also draft the script, pages, emails, polls and offers from one description of your webinar.

How does a webinar sales funnel work?

A webinar sales funnel moves people from the registration page to the session, then to the offer and checkout. EasyWebinar's analytics count each step, from visitors and registrations to offer views, clicks and purchases.

Can I take payments during a webinar?

Yes. An EasyWebinar offer button opens your own checkout, booking or sales page. For paid registration, attendees pay through a Stripe checkout and the money goes straight to your Stripe account.

Do attendees need to download anything?

No. Every EasyWebinar plan includes live webinars with browser join, so attendees click their link and watch in the browser. Contacts on your list can register through a one-click link that skips the form.

Will EasyWebinar work with my email tool?

EasyWebinar connects to tools including HubSpot, ActiveCampaign, Mailchimp, AWeber and Kit (formerly ConvertKit). It can tag contacts as attended, missed or clicked offer. You can also keep contacts in EasyCRM instead.

Is there a free trial, and do I need a card?

EasyWebinar has no free plan. The standard trial is 7 days and requires a card. Plans are priced flat monthly, with no per-attendee fees, and current prices are on the pricing page.

Which plan do I need for an automated sales webinar?

Every EasyWebinar plan includes live webinars. Automation, EasyCRM and checkout start at the Growth plan, so an evergreen or simulive sales webinar needs Growth or above.

What should I send people who missed it?

Send no-shows the replay link as soon as the session ends. In EasyWebinar, add a Webinar Follow-up with its audience set to no-shows. A replay set to Webinar Replica repeats the live offers.
