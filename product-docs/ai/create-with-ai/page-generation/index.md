## What an AI-generated webinar landing page covers {#what-it-is}

Picture a coach with a topic, an offer and a date in mind, but no registration page yet. She can write one from a blank screen tonight, or she can describe the session and let the AI draft it. An AI-generated webinar landing page is the second route: pages written by AI from a description of your webinar, which you then edit like any other page.

In EasyWebinar, page generation is one part of a larger AI build that starts from a single prompt. The AI writes your presentation script first. It then writes the pages, using that script as its source material. Because the build starts from one description, the pages and the talk describe the same session.

The AI covers three of the five pages every webinar has: the registration page, the thank-you page and the waiting area. The other two, the webinar page and the replay page, keep the working default that every page has. All five open in the same drag-and-drop builder, so you can style the last two whenever you're ready.

### What you see on the screen {#what-you-see}

During the build, the pages open in an editor with four tabs: **Edit**, **Preview**, **SEO** and **Scripts**. A desktop and mobile view lets you check both layouts before you move on. When the build ends, you land on the finished webinar's **Overview**, and the webinar carries an **AI** badge in **My Webinars**. From then on, its pages sit on the **Content** tab and open in the [registration page builder](https://maximuslabs-ai.github.io/easywebinar-product-docs/product-docs/webinars/registration/registration-page-builder/), exactly as they would on a webinar you built by hand.

## How webinar landing pages get made {#how-pages-get-made}

There are four common ways to get a webinar landing page live. Each one catches some problems and misses others, so it helps to know which route you're on before you judge the result.

### Built by hand on a blank page {#blank-page}

You control every word and block, and nothing appears that you didn't write. What it misses is speed. The thank-you and waiting pages need writing too, and a busy team often leaves them on their defaults.

### Started from a ready-made template {#from-a-template}

Webinar landing page templates fix the layout and design quickly, with the form already in place. What they miss is the words. Until you replace the sample copy, a template says nothing about your own session.

### Written by AI from your script {#written-by-ai}

The copy describes your actual session, because it's drawn from the script for the talk. That catches the gap between what the webinar covers and what the page promises. What it can miss are facts the AI may get wrong or can't know, such as the price, what's included and anything you changed after the script was written.

### A form embedded on your own site {#embedded-form}

The page stays on your own website, in your own design, with the webinar's registration form placed inside it. That keeps the brand consistent. What it misses is the copy around the form, which is still yours to write.

| Page | When people see it | Written by the AI? | What to check |

## How much of the page you'll change {#how-much-to-change}

Expect to edit, not rebuild. The product's own guidance treats the generated pages as a strong first draft, and it singles out pricing and what's included as the things to read closely. The checks below take most people well under an hour, because the layout and most of the copy are already there.

### Webinar landing page best practices to check {#best-practices}

| Check | Why it matters | Where you change it |
|---|---|---|
| Price and what's included | The AI may describe the offer differently from how you sell it | The registration page copy |
| Date and time | The pages show the schedule, which you set after the build | The schedule, then **Preview** |
| Headline | Most visitors scan rather than read | The top block of the registration page |
| Form fields | The default asks for **Full name** and **Email**, both required | The form block's settings |
| Mobile layout | Most of your registrations will come from phones | The device toggle in the builder |
| Search and sharing | Controls what search results and shared links show | **SEO Options** and **Social Sharing (Open Graph)** |
| Ad tracking | Lets your ad platform see sign-ups | **Tracking Code** on each page |

Read the headline the way a visitor would. In Nielsen Norman Group's early research, [79 percent of test users always scanned](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-users-read-on-the-web/) a new page instead of reading word by word. Its later eyetracking work advises putting [the most important points in the first two paragraphs](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/f-shaped-pattern-reading-web-content/) and starting headings with the words that carry most information. If the generated headline opens with filler, move the promise to the front.

Be slow to add form fields. The same group's form guidance notes that [every field you cut raises a form's conversion rate](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/web-form-design/), and it suggests keeping optional fields to one or two. Add a [custom registration field](https://maximuslabs-ai.github.io/easywebinar-product-docs/product-docs/webinars/registration/custom-fields/) only when you'll actually use the answer.

For layout ideas beyond the generated draft, the blog's [webinar landing page examples](https://easywebinar.com/blog/webinar-landing-page-examples-that-convert/) and its guide to [high-conversion landing page essentials](https://easywebinar.com/blog/high-conversion-webinar-landing-page-essentials/) are worth a look before you edit.

## Write a prompt that gives the pages something to say {#write-the-prompt}

The pages are only as specific as the prompt behind them. A weak prompt produces generic output, and the generic wording flows through the script into every page. Include these five things:

- **Who it's for, specifically.** "Owners of young dogs that pull on the lead" gives the AI more than "dog owners".
- **What it covers.** Name the three or four things you'll actually teach.
- **How long it runs.**
- **The tone you want.**
- **What you're selling at the end, and the price.**

A prompt along these lines gives the pages plenty to work with:

> Build a 45-minute live session called "Loose-Lead Walking in Four Weeks" for owners of young, energetic dogs who have already tried harnesses and treats. Cover why dogs pull, the stop-and-wait method, choosing a lead and practising on busy streets. Warm and practical. End with an offer for a four-week group programme with weekly calls.

Read the script before you continue to the pages. It's much easier to fix the script at this point than to fix the pages and emails built from it. If you want the price on the page, add it to your prompt, then check it on the page before you publish.

## How to measure a webinar landing page {#measure}

Two measures tell you whether a landing page is doing its job. Both are about the page itself, not about the webinar that follows.

### Registration rate {#registration-rate}

Registration rate is registered people divided by visitors. In the analytics funnel, **Visitors** counts unique IPs on your landing page and **Registered** counts unique sign-ups. A low rate here is a page problem: the headline, the form or the promise.

Its weak spots are worth knowing. Counting by IP means several people on one office network may show as a single visitor. With small numbers, a handful of sign-ups can swing the rate a long way. Compare a session with your own earlier sessions rather than with a figure from elsewhere. You'll find both numbers in [webinar analytics](https://maximuslabs-ai.github.io/easywebinar-product-docs/product-docs/webinars/analytics/webinar-analytics/).

### Sign-ups your ad platform can see {#thank-you-signal}

The thank-you page only loads after someone registers, which makes it a reliable conversion signal. Tracking code is set per page: the registration page tells your ad platform who was interested, and the thank-you page tells it who converted. Its weak spot is that it counts sign-ups, not attendance. A low **Registered** to **Attended** number is a reminder problem rather than a page one.

If you run search ads to the page, Google Ads rates [landing page experience](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6167118) as one of three parts of Quality Score: how relevant and useful the page is to people who click the ad. Google calls Quality Score a diagnostic tool, not a key performance indicator. Treat it as a hint about the page, and keep registration rate as your main number.

## How to create a webinar landing page by hand {#manual-method}

Here's the manual method, so you can see which steps the AI takes on.

1. Write one sentence on who the webinar is for and what they'll leave with.
2. Turn that sentence into a headline that leads with the words carrying most information.
3. List the three or four things attendees will learn.
4. Write a short presenter bio that says why you're the right person to teach this.
5. Add the date, time and timezone, and a registration form that asks only what you need.
6. Write a thank-you page that confirms the booking and says what happens next.
7. Write a waiting page for people who arrive early.
8. Check every page on a phone, then publish it and test the registration link.

### The same job in EasyWebinar {#same-job-in-easywebinar}

Steps 1 to 4 come from your prompt and script: the AI writes the headline, learning points and bio from them. Step 5 comes from the webinar itself, because the pages carry its schedule and the default form asks for name and email. The AI writes steps 6 and 7 in the same pass as the registration page. Step 8 stays with you: use the device toggle and **Preview**, then **Publish** each page.

### Worked example: reading the dog trainer's first registration rate {#worked-example}

This is an illustrative calculation with sample figures, not an output from the product. It shows how to read the funnel once the generated pages have been live for one session.

| Measure | Sample figure | How it's worked out |
|---|---|---|
| Visitors to the registration page | 600 | Unique IPs, from the funnel |
| Registered | 90 | Unique sign-ups |
| Registration rate | 15% | 90 ÷ 600 = 0.15 |
| Live attended | 36 | People who joined the live broadcast |
| Registered to attended | 40% | 36 ÷ 90 = 0.40 |

The first rate is the page's job and the second belongs to the reminders. If her 15% looked low against her own past sessions, she'd edit the headline, the form or the promise. If attendance looked low instead, she'd change her [reminder emails](https://maximuslabs-ai.github.io/easywebinar-product-docs/product-docs/webinars/notifications/reminder-emails/) and leave the page alone.

#### Can I choose the webinar landing page template myself? {#faq-choose-template}

Yes. Choose **Choose manually** to browse templates, or **AI picks for you** to have one selected. Pick before detailed editing, because changing a template replaces that page's layout.

#### Do I need to type the date and time into the page? {#faq-date-time}

No. Every webinar page carries the webinar's date, time and timezone automatically. You do still need to set the schedule after the AI build, so the pages show the right date.

#### Is my registration link live as soon as the build finishes? {#faq-link-live}

Not until you publish. Each page has its own **Publish** button, and saving alone keeps a draft. The registration link doesn't work until the registration page is published.

#### Will the generated pages work on phones? {#faq-mobile}

The editor has a desktop and mobile view, and the builder has a device toggle. Check the mobile layout before you publish, because most registrations tend to come from phones.

#### Can I generate the pages without emails, polls or offers? {#faq-pages-only}

Yes. Turn off any of the output chips before you click **Generate**. Script, Landing pages and Emails are on by default, and Polls and Offers are off until you select them.

#### Does it work for evergreen and simulive webinars? {#faq-webinar-types}

Yes. Set **Type** to Evergreen or Simulive before generating. You'll still choose the video afterwards, and you'll set the schedule as you would for any webinar.

#### How long does the build take? {#faq-how-long}

About ten minutes end to end for the full build. The script is the slowest part, at roughly a minute and a half.

#### Can I put the registration form on my own website? {#faq-own-website}

Yes. On the **Content** tab, the **Registration Widget** gives you embed code to paste into your own site. People can then register without visiting the generated page.

#### What should I check before publishing a generated page? {#faq-check-before-publish}

Read the copy for pricing and what's included, then set the schedule and confirm the date. Check the mobile layout, the form fields and the SEO and sharing settings, then publish.
