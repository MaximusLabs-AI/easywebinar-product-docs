Every EasyWebinar webinar has an Analytics tab that shows how many people reached each stage of that webinar and where the rest dropped away. It has three views: Overview for the funnel, engagement and offer numbers, People for the individuals behind those numbers, and Revenue for what the webinar earned. A date range at the top limits everything on the page to the period you choose.

01 · Measure

What webinar analytics is

The Analytics tab is the per-webinar report. It answers two questions: how many people got through each stage, from landing on the registration page to watching, and where the others stopped.

The Overview view is built around a funnel. People arrive at the registration page, some register, some of those attend live or watch the replay, and some never show up. Below the funnel sit the numbers for what happened during the session: chat, questions, and how your offers performed.

The People view lists who registered and who attended. The Revenue view breaks down what the webinar earned, separating paid registrations from offers sold in the room.

For a quick read without opening the tab, the webinar's Overview tab shows four live stats (Registrants, Live Attended, Total Attended and Revenue) that update as the webinar runs. See The Overview tab.

02 · Why it matters

Why webinar analytics matters

What it's for

Analytics counts how far people get through a webinar, from the page they land on to the offer at the end. Each stage of the funnel is controlled by its own part of your setup. So a weak number tells you which part to work on: the page, the reminders, the talk, the offer or the checkout.

How it helps

  • It locates the leak. Visitors to Registered measures your registration page. Registered to attended measures your reminder emails.
  • It separates offer problems from checkout problems. Views to Clicks and Clicks to Purchases show where buyers drop out.
  • It shows who to follow up. Missed is everyone who registered and never attended.
  • It ties money to the webinar. Webinar revenue sits next to attendance, so you can see what each session earned.

What happens without it

Without per-stage numbers, a disappointing webinar is just disappointing. You can't tell whether few people saw the page, the page didn't convert, the reminders failed or the offer fell flat. So you end up changing everything at once. It's easy to rewrite a talk that was fine, when the real problem was that people never turned up.

Why buyers care

It matters most when a webinar carries a target. That might be sign-ups for a marketing team, enrolments for a course creator or paid seats for a workshop host. It earns its keep in the days after a session, when you choose what to change before promoting the next one.

03 · Our approach

The EasyWebinar approach to webinar analytics

Consider a typical case. A B2B marketing team runs a monthly webinar for prospects, and each session closes with an offer button to book a demo. Registrations rose this quarter, but demo bookings stayed flat. The team has to show where the extra registrants went.

A common approach is to rebuild the funnel from exports. Sign-ups come from the form tool, attendance from the meeting tool and bookings from the sales calendar, and someone lines them up in a spreadsheet. That can take days each month. Names may not match across three lists, and a person who watched live and then on the recording can be counted twice.

Every stage up to the demo button happens on the webinar platform: the page visit, the sign-up, the session, the recording and the click. A platform that runs all of those can count every stage for the same webinar, so nobody has to merge lists by hand.

EasyWebinar's Analytics tab does that count for each webinar. Its Overview view runs from Visitors and Registered through Live Attended and Replay Watched. Further down, Offers Performance reports each offer's views and clicks. For this team, that shows whether the extra registrants stopped before the session, during it, or at the demo button.

The counts are built to avoid double counting. Visitors counts unique IPs on the landing page. Total Attended counts each person once and files them under Live only, Replay only or Both. How other platforms report on a single webinar is covered in How webinar analytics compares, below.

04 · Measure

Read your analytics

1. Open the tab and set the period

Open your webinar and click Analytics. It opens on Overview.

1 Overview, People and Revenue · 2 Date range and Apply · 3 Funnel metrics.

Set the date range and click Apply. The range applies to every number on the page, so if a webinar looks emptier than you expected, widen it first.

2. Read the funnel

MetricWhat it counts
VisitorsUnique IPs on your landing page
RegisteredUnique sign-ups
Live AttendedPeople who joined the live broadcast
Replay WatchedPeople who watched the recording
Total AttendedUnique people, live or replay, split into Live only, Replay only and Both
MissedRegistered and never showed up

Live Attended covers the broadcast only. Total Attended counts each person once whether they watched live, on replay or both, which is why it's the number to use when you ask how many people the webinar reached.

3. Find where people drop off

Two gaps tell you most of what you need:

GapWhat it measuresIf it's low, look at
Visitors to RegisteredYour registration pageThe headline, the form and the promise on the page
Registered to attendedYour remindersConfirmation emails and reminders, not the content

Missed is the group most worth contacting after a session. Send them the replay.

4. Check engagement and offers

Further down the Overview:

PanelWhat it shows
Offers PerformanceEach offer's views, clicks and purchases, with a click rate
Views to Clicks and Clicks to PurchasesWhere your offer loses people
Revenue by OfferTotal revenue and payment count, with a details link
ChatTotal messages, which you can filter by session
Questions & AnswersHow many questions were asked and how many you answered
Registrations by countryWhere your audience is

Read Views to Clicks and Clicks to Purchases together. A low views-to-clicks rate means the offer itself isn't landing. A low clicks-to-purchases rate means people want the offer but your checkout or price is stopping them.

After a busy session, the Not Answered Rate on Q&A tells you how many questions you left behind. Those are worth answering in your follow-up email.

5. See the people behind the numbers

Switch to People to see who registered and who attended. For a full list across all your webinars, with columns such as watch percentage, offer clicked and lead score, plus filters and export, use People in the sidebar.

6. Check revenue

Switch to Revenue.

AreaWhat it shows
1RevenueThe revenue view for this webinar
2Total Revenue and Paid RegistrationsEverything earned, and the part that came from registrations
3Revenue by SourceThe split between paid registrations and offers sold in the room
4Paid WebinarSwitches the section below to registration payments: completed, pending and abandoned checkouts, and every transaction

Offer sales appear under Paid Offer in the same view, so registration revenue and in-room sales are kept apart. The transaction list shows each buyer's name, email, session, registration date, amount and status, and you can search it and filter by status.

Transactions show one of these statuses: Pending, Processing, Success, Failed, Canceled, Refunded or Partially Refunded. Checkout abandoned counts people who reached checkout but didn't pay; they aren't registered. A webinar with no payments shows "No transactions yet."

05 · How it works

What to do with each number

What you seeWhat it usually meansWhere to act
Few VisitorsNot enough promotion reaching the pageYour campaigns and the Registration Page Link
Low Visitors to RegisteredThe page isn't persuadingRegistration page builder
Low Registered to attendedPeople forget or lose the linkReminder emails
High MissedA large audience still to reachA follow-up to no-shows with the replay link

Low Clicks to PurchasesInterest without purchaseYour checkout page or price

06 · Measure

How analytics works with other features

Follow-up emails

Webinar Follow-ups on the Notifications tab can go to everyone who registered, only attendees, or only no-shows, which matches the Missed number directly. Attendee Action Follow-ups send based on engagement and whether someone clicked an offer.

CRM and email tools

A connected CRM can tag people by the same behaviour Analytics counts: Registered, Attended, Missed, Watched replay, Watched full and Clicked offer, among others. That lets your sales or email system act on what the numbers show. See Connect a CRM and map your webinar data.

Offers and countdowns

Offers Performance reports on the offers you build on the CTAs tab. Comparing sessions is the clearest way to see whether a change, such as adding a countdown timer, helped.

Paid registration

For paid webinars, the Revenue view is where every registration payment and its status appear. See Paid webinars.

Replays

Replay Watched and the Replay only and Both splits only fill in if people can reach your recording. See Replay pages.

07 · Measure

Who uses webinar analytics, and how

These are typical patterns, shown to illustrate how different teams read the same tab.

B2B marketing teams

A demand generation team runs a monthly webinar and tracks Visitors to Registered after every promotion change. When the rate drops, they test a new registration page headline before changing anything else.

Course creators

A creator selling a course through an evergreen webinar watches Offers Performance and Clicks to Purchases. A healthy click rate with few purchases sends them to the checkout page, not the presentation. See Course creators.

Coaches and consultants

A coach running paid workshops checks the Revenue view after each session, compares Paid Registrations with offer sales under Revenue by Source, and keeps an eye on Checkout abandoned.

SaaS and product teams

A product team running onboarding webinars cares most about Total Attended and Replay only, because many users catch up on the recording. A high Not Answered Rate tells them to go back through the questions and cover the gaps in their follow-up.

Why EasyWebinar is the stronger choice for webinar analytics

  • Offer results. EasyWebinar's Offers Performance shows views, clicks and purchases for each offer. Demio's Featured Action shows clicks and the share of attendees who clicked, and its docs describe no purchase tracking. See EasyWebinar vs Demio.
  • Revenue next to attendance. EasyWebinar's Revenue view splits paid sign-ups from offers sold in the room. ON24's report docs show no revenue figure, and Livestorm's analytics docs show none either. See EasyWebinar vs ON24 and EasyWebinar vs Livestorm.
  • Live and replay in one count. EasyWebinar's Total Attended shows unique people, split into Live only, Replay only and Both. WebinarJam shows live and replay data separately, and Zoom reports recording views on Webinars Plus. See EasyWebinar vs WebinarJam and EverWebinar and EasyWebinar vs Zoom.
  • One date range for the whole tab. Set a date range, click Apply, and every number on EasyWebinar's tab follows it. WebinarJam filters by webinar, session and event, with no custom date range described.

09 · Limits

Limits and things to know

  • Analytics is per webinar. The People page in the sidebar is the one that spans every webinar.
  • The date range covers the whole page. Widen it if numbers look low.
  • A brand-new webinar shows zeros until registrations start.
  • Visitors are unique IPs on your landing page.
  • Replay views are counted separately from live attendance; someone who did both appears under Both.
  • Registration and offer revenue are reported separately, under Paid Webinar and Paid Offer.
  • An abandoned checkout isn't a registration.
10 · Questions

Common questions

What's the difference between Live Attended and Total Attended?

Live Attended is the live broadcast only. Total Attended counts unique people who watched live or on replay.

How do I see how many people didn't turn up?

Use Missed: people who registered and never attended.

Why is everything showing zero?

Either the webinar hasn't run yet, or your date range doesn't cover the period when it did.

How do I know if my offer worked?

Check Offers Performance for views, clicks and purchases, and Revenue by Offer for what it earned.

Where do I see who registered?

In the People view on the Analytics tab, or in People in the sidebar for every webinar at once.

Can I see where my audience is?

Yes. Registrations by country is at the bottom of the Overview.

How do I see which questions I didn't answer?

Questions & Answers shows how many were asked and answered, and the Not Answered Rate shows how many you left behind.

Are registration payments and offer sales reported together?

No. Revenue by Source splits them, and the section below switches between Paid Webinar and Paid Offer.

What does Checkout abandoned mean?

People who reached the checkout for a paid webinar but didn't pay. They aren't registered.

Does replay viewing count as attendance?

It counts towards Total Attended. Live Attended counts the broadcast only.
