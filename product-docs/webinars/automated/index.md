An automated webinar plays a recording at the times your registrants choose, so one good session can run all week without you presenting it again. ## What an automated webinar is {#what-it-is}

Say you run the same product walkthrough every week, and each run is nearly word for word the last one. Moving it on to a recording frees that hour, and lets people who can't make your slot still watch.

An automated webinar is a webinar whose content is a recording rather than a live presenter. The platform plays that recording at scheduled times, and registrants join a session the way they would join a live one. In EasyWebinar, two of the three webinar types work this way: **Simulive** and **Evergreen**. A **Live** webinar needs no recording, because you supply the content by presenting. [Automated webinars on EasyWebinar](https://easywebinar.com/automated-webinars/) gives the product overview.

## Why automated webinars matter {#why-it-matters}

### What it's for {#what-its-for}

An automated webinar lets a session you deliver often keep running without you in the room. People who find your registration page can watch soon, at a time that suits them, instead of waiting for your next live slot.

### How it helps {#how-it-helps}

- **More sessions, same effort.** One recording fills a daily or weekly schedule with no extra presenting.
- **Less waiting.** A just-in-time session starts a few minutes after someone registers.
- **Times that fit your audience.** Registrants pick a session, and just-in-time sessions follow their local time.
- **The same room tools.** Chat, Q&A, polls, offers, emails and analytics work as they do on a live webinar.
- **Easy updates.** You can swap the recording later without touching the schedule or the registrations.

### What happens without it {#without-it}

Every session needs you live, at one fixed time. Someone who registers on a Tuesday for a Thursday slot has two days to lose interest. Anyone in a distant timezone may never find a slot at all. Your reach stops at the number of hours you can present.

### Why buyers care {#why-buyers-care}

This matters most to SaaS teams, marketers and course creators. For them, the webinar is a step in a funnel, such as a demo, an onboarding session or an offer. It matters most when a live session already works and the next question is how to run it more often.

## How an automated webinar delivers a session {#how-delivery-works}

Every automated webinar plays a recording. What differs is when each session starts and who watches it together. Each way below catches a different kind of registrant and misses another.

### Simulive: set times, one shared room {#simulive}

A [simulive webinar](https://maximuslabs-ai.github.io/easywebinar-product-docs/product-docs/webinars/getting-started/create-a-simulive-webinar/) broadcasts the recording at times you set, like a scheduled television programme. Everyone in a session sees the same moment, so the chat works as a shared room, and you can answer questions live while the recording plays. It catches people who value the event feeling. It misses anyone who can't make your times, because attendees register for specific dates.

### Evergreen recurring schedule: set times that repeat {#recurring-schedule}

An evergreen webinar can run **Daily** or **Weekly** at the times you choose, with **Add Time** for more than one slot a day. These sessions run in the webinar's timezone. This catches people who like to plan ahead. It misses people who arrive keen right now and face a long wait.

### Just-in-time: a session starting shortly {#just-in-time}

[Just-in-time sessions](https://maximuslabs-ai.github.io/easywebinar-product-docs/product-docs/webinars/automated/just-in-time/) start a few minutes after someone registers, in their own local time. The **Interval** setting decides how often they come round, and **Exclude specific time** keeps chosen hours clear. This catches the visitor at the moment of interest. It suits a funnel, though people arriving at the same minute are rarely numerous, so the room is usually small.

### On demand: watch straight away {#on-demand}

[On Demand](https://maximuslabs-ai.github.io/easywebinar-product-docs/product-docs/webinars/on-demand/) lets a registrant start the recording immediately, with no session to wait for. The button reads *Watch instant replay* by default, and you can reword it. It catches people who want the content and nothing else. It gives up the sense of a scheduled event, so leave it off when you want people to turn up.

EasyWebinar is confirmed to run simulive webinars, and evergreen webinars with any mix of just-in-time, on-demand and recurring sessions. In an evergreen webinar, your registrant picks from the options you switch on.

## Live vs automated webinar: which to run {#live-vs-automated}

Pick by what your audience came for. If the value is you being there, run it live. If the value is the content, a recording will do.

| If you want | Choose |
|---|---|
| To present in real time and answer questions as they come | **Live** |
| A polished recording at set times, with you in the chat | **Simulive** |
| A session running continuously, with each registrant picking a time | **Evergreen** |
| The same session weekly without presenting it again | **Simulive** or **Evergreen** |
| To record once and let the session sell on its own | **Evergreen** |

A common path is to run the session live a few times to sharpen it, then clone it as Evergreen. [Create and schedule a live webinar](https://maximuslabs-ai.github.io/easywebinar-product-docs/product-docs/webinars/getting-started/create-a-live-webinar/) covers the live side, and EasyWebinar's post on [live vs automated vs evergreen webinars](https://easywebinar.com/blog/live-vs-automated-webinars/) goes further into the choice.

## Plan the schedule around your audience's calendar {#plan-the-calendar}

A schedule set once keeps running until you stop it, so check it against the calendar your registrants live by.

Clock changes shift recurring sessions for some viewers. Set times run in the webinar's timezone, so a session fixed in UK time moves by an hour for viewers whose clocks change on other dates. In the UK, the clocks [went back on 25 October 2026 and go forward on 28 March 2027](https://www.gov.uk/when-do-the-clocks-change), according to GOV.UK. Just-in-time sessions avoid this, because they follow each attendee's local time.

Holidays are the other check. GOV.UK lists [25 and 28 December 2026 as bank holidays in England and Wales](https://www.gov.uk/bank-holidays), with 28 December as the Boxing Day substitute day. If nobody will attend then, add those dates under **Blackout Dates** so the webinar doesn't run.

## How to measure an automated webinar {#measuring}

Two measures tell you whether the schedule is doing its job.

The first is **show-up rate**: attendees divided by registrants. It shows whether your session times suit people. Its weak spot is that on-demand viewers start at once, so a high on-demand share can make the scheduled sessions look stronger or weaker than they are. Compare scheduled and on-demand viewers separately.

The second is **time to first watch**: how long a registrant waits between signing up and the session starting. Shorter waits are the reason just-in-time exists, and HBR's research on online leads found that [most companies are not responding nearly fast enough](https://hbr.org/2011/03/the-short-life-of-online-sales-leads) to people who show interest. Its weak spot is that you work it out from your schedule, not from a single number.

## How to run a session on a repeating schedule by hand {#manual-method}

Without automation, keeping a session available means doing the scheduling yourself.

1. Record the session, or keep the recording of a live run.
2. Pick the days and times you will run it, and publish a page listing them.
3. Collect registrations and send each person the link for their slot.
4. Start the recording at each slot yourself, or present it live again.
5. Skip holidays by hand, and move slots when clocks change.
6. Send a replay link to anyone who missed their time.

Every step repeats for every session, and anyone who arrives between slots waits for the next one.

### The same job in EasyWebinar {#same-job-in-easywebinar}

EasyWebinar does steps 2 to 6 from the schedule step. You choose the recording once, switch on the ways to watch, and set a start date with **No end date** if it should run indefinitely. The sessions list then fills itself: a daily schedule generates a long run of upcoming sessions without you adding any. **Blackout Dates** handles the holidays, and On Demand covers the person who would otherwise wait.

### Worked example: one week of a weekday schedule {#worked-example}

This is an illustrative calculation with sample figures, not an EasyWebinar output. A team runs its walkthrough **Weekly** on Monday to Friday at 11:00 and 16:00, and expects 300 registrants that week.

| Step | Working | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Set sessions in a normal week | 5 days × 2 times | 10 sessions |
| Blackout Dates in Christmas week | 25 and 28 December removed, 2 days × 2 times | 4 sessions removed |
| Set sessions left that week | 10 − 4 | 6 sessions |
| Registrants per set session, all on set times | 300 ÷ 6 | 50 per session |
| If 40% of registrants choose On Demand | 300 × 40% | 120 watch at once |
| Registrants left for set sessions | 300 − 120 | 180, or 30 per session |

The blackout dates cut the week's set sessions by 40%, so each remaining session gets busier. Turning on On Demand spreads that load and takes the waiting out for a large share of people. Turning on just-in-time would do the same for those who still want a session.

### Create the webinar {#create-the-webinar}

1. On **My Webinars**, click **Create a Webinar**, then **Create Manually**.
2. Give it a name, choose **Evergreen Webinar**, and click **Continue to Schedule**.

### Set your schedule {#set-your-schedule}

1. Turn on any mix of **Just-in-time Registration**, **On Demand** and **Recurring Schedule**.
2. For just-in-time, set the **Interval** to every 2, 15, 30, 45 or 60 minutes, and use **Exclude specific time** for hours to keep clear.
3. For the recurring schedule, choose **Daily** or **Weekly** and add times with **Add Time**.
4. Under **Availability**, set the **Start Date**, and leave **End Date** as **No end date** to run indefinitely.
5. Under **Schedule Preferences**, choose how many options a registrant sees, and add any **Blackout Dates**.

## Limits and things to know {#limits}

- **The type is fixed.** You can't change a webinar's type after creating it; clone it instead.
- **Simulive and evergreen need a recording.** Only live webinars run without one.
- **Set times use the webinar's timezone.** Daily and weekly sessions don't shift to each attendee's local time; only just-in-time sessions do.
- **No zooming in on shared content.** Attendees of a live room can zoom in on a shared screen or file, but automated webinars use a separate attendee page where that isn't available.
- **A recurring schedule runs until you stop it.** Set an **End Date**, or use **Blackout Dates** to skip days.

## Common questions {#common-questions}

#### How does an automated webinar work? {#faq-how-it-works}

You choose a recording and a schedule. EasyWebinar then plays the recording at each session time, and registrants join as they would a live webinar. On an evergreen webinar, each registrant picks from the just-in-time, on-demand or set times you switch on.

#### What is the difference between a live and an automated webinar? {#faq-live-vs-automated}

A live webinar is presented in real time at one fixed date and time. An automated webinar plays a recording instead, either at set times (simulive) or on a repeating schedule each registrant chooses from (evergreen).

#### What is webinar automation? {#faq-webinar-automation}

Webinar automation means the platform runs your sessions from a recording, on a schedule, instead of you presenting each one. In EasyWebinar, simulive and evergreen webinars work this way, and chat, Q&A, polls, offers and emails still run in the room.

#### How do I create an automated webinar? {#faq-how-to-create}

On **My Webinars**, click **Create a Webinar**, then **Create Manually**. Choose **Evergreen Webinar** or **Simulive Webinar**, set the schedule and pricing, pick the recording on the video step, then choose a registration template and publish the page.

#### What's the difference between simulive and evergreen? {#faq-simulive-vs-evergreen}

Both play a recording. Simulive broadcasts it at times you set, so everyone in a session watches together and you can chat live. Evergreen runs on a repeating schedule and lets each registrant choose a time that suits them.

#### Do I need YouTube or Vimeo to run an automated webinar? {#faq-video-hosting}

No. Upload your file with **Local import** and EasyWebinar hosts the video in your library. Importing from YouTube, Vimeo, Wistia or a direct MP4 link is an option if the video is already hosted there.

#### Can I turn a live webinar into an automated one? {#faq-live-to-automated}

Yes. Clone the live webinar, choose Evergreen on the copy, and select the recording of the live session on the video step. Many people run a session live a few times first, then clone it this way.

#### Can I change the recording after the webinar is running? {#faq-change-recording}

Yes. Open the **Webinar Video** panel on the **Content** tab and choose another video. Your schedule and your existing registrations stay as they were.

#### What timezone do automated sessions run in? {#faq-timezone}

Just-in-time sessions start shortly after each person registers, in their own local time. Daily and weekly sessions run at the times you set, in the webinar's timezone, so check those times against clock changes.

#### How often do just-in-time sessions start? {#faq-jit-interval}

You set this with the **Interval** setting on the schedule step: every 2, 15, 30, 45 or 60 minutes. **Exclude specific time** keeps chosen hours free of just-in-time sessions.

#### Can I stop an automated webinar running on holidays? {#faq-blackout}

Yes. In **Schedule Preferences**, turn on **Blackout Dates** and pick the days to skip. The webinar won't run on those dates, and the rest of the schedule carries on.

#### Can I chat with attendees during an automated webinar? {#faq-chat}

Yes. On a simulive webinar, the recording plays while you and your moderators answer in the chat live. Chat and Q&A also work on evergreen webinars, and Chat Simulation (Beta) can make the room feel populated.

#### Can attendees zoom in on slides in an automated webinar? {#faq-zoom-in}

No. Attendees can zoom in on a shared screen or file in a live room, but automated webinars use a separate attendee page, and zooming in isn't available there.

#### Is my registration page public as soon as I create the webinar? {#faq-publish}

No. The webinar opens in the registration page builder with your schedule applied, but the page goes public only when you click **Publish**. Use **Preview** to check it first.
